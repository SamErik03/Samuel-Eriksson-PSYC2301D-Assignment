For Carleton and Ottawa university students, 18 to 24

Two drinks a week is Canada's new low-risk line.

In 2023, Canada's alcohol guidance dropped from 10–15 drinks a week to 2.1,6 Only about 45% of post-secondary students have heard of it.5 Where does your week land?

0 drinksNo risk

Not drinking has its own benefits, like better health and better sleep.

1–2 a weekLow risk

You'll likely avoid alcohol-related harm. Lifetime risk of dying early from alcohol is about 1 in 1,000.

3–6 a weekModerate risk

Risk of several cancers rises, including breast and colon. Lifetime risk of early death is about 1 in 100.

7+ a weekIncreasingly high risk

Risk of heart disease and stroke rises too, and each extra drink pushes it higher.

Risk levels from Canada's Guidance on Alcohol and Health.2,7

Drinking is the norm on campus. Knowing the risks isn't.

Health Canada surveyed 22,961 students aged 17–25 at 43 Canadian colleges and universities between October 2024 and April 2025.3 Alcohol is still the substance students use most. Fewer than half know the guidance that explains its risks.

"Heavy drinking" means 4 or more drinks (females) or 5 or more drinks (males) on one occasion.5

The science moved. The limit moved with it.

The 2011 guidelines called up to 10 drinks a week (women) or 15 (men) "low risk." The 2023 Canada's Guidance on Alcohol and Health is based on a review of more recent evidence. It found the risk starts much lower and gives the same advice to everyone.1

Two drinks a week keeps your lifetime risk of dying early from alcohol at about 1 in 1,000. At 3–6 drinks a week, that risk rises to about 1 in 100.7

One drink is smaller than you think.

Canada defines a standard drink by its size and strength. Each of these counts as one:2

The cost shows up the next morning.

Among students who drank in the past year, 45% had at least one harm from their own drinking in the last 30 days. The most common ones:5

Lost energy and hangovers take time away from studying, sports and sleep. The guidance lists better sleep as one benefit of not drinking.2

And it adds up over years.

  • Alcohol is classified as a Group 1 carcinogen, meaning it is known to cause cancer.8
  • At 3–6 drinks a week, the risk of several cancers rises, including breast and colon cancer. At 7 or more, the risk of heart disease and stroke rises too.2
  • Only 29% of Canadian drinkers surveyed in 2023 knew alcohol causes seven types of cancer.9
  • In 2020, alcohol caused 17,098 deaths in Canada and cost about $19.7 billion.10,11

You don't have to quit to lower your risk.

The guidance says the same thing at every level: any drink you cut lowers your risk.1 Four habits that fit student life:

Count

Keep track of your drinks for a week, using standard drinks, not cans. Most people have more than they guess.

Cap

Have no more than 2 drinks on any one day. Going over that raises your risk of injury and of harming someone else.7

Swap

Alternate each drink with water or a non-alcoholic one, and eat before and while you drink.

Zero days

Plan days with no drinking every week, so it doesn't turn into a habit.

Fewer is better. Know your two.

Count your drinks in standard drinks. Keep any day to 2 or fewer. Aim for 0–2 a week, the lowest-risk zone in Canada's guidance. Every drink you skip lowers your risk.
